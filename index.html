01 — Executive Overview

Augmenting
Railway ATP

Traditional Automatic Train Protection (ATP) systems such as Indian Railways Kavach (TCAS) rely on fixed track circuits, axle counters, and RFID transponders to prevent Signal Passed at Danger (SPAD) and train collisions. However, they are inherently blind to unexpected physical track obstructions — including deliberate rail sabotage (placed girders, rods, fishplates), fallen storm trees, boulders, stalled level-crossing vehicles, and human trespassers.

Drishti-Kavach (Hindi: Shield of Vision) introduces an autonomous forward-looking optical perception envelope. By decoupling high-speed pixel-dense track gauge segmentation from localized physical hazard detection, and cross-referencing their spatial coordinates through polygon vector geometry, it provides instantaneous driver warning and automatic emergency brake triggering.

02 — Architecture & Workflow

Decoupled Perception
& Spatial Reasoning

Drishti-Kavach solves the latency-accuracy tradeoff through a decoupled dual-engine architecture: a lightweight bilateral segmentation network isolates the drivable rail gauge while a fine-tuned detector localizes hazards in parallel.

Step 01 OPTICAL
Optical Enhancement
Captures forward 1080p feeds under Daylight RGB or Active 850nm NIR. Applies Adaptive CLAHE and Dark Channel Prior (DCP) defogging for zero-light and fog conditions.
src/7_pipeline/weather_enhancer.py
Step 02 SEGMENT
BiSeNetV2 Segmentation
Lightweight bilateral network with Detail and Semantic branches generates pixel-level masks for Drivable Trackbed (89.5% IoU) and Steel Rails at 512×1024 resolution.
src/2_models/bisenetv2.py
Step 03 DETECT
YOLO11m Hazard Detector
High-precision 8-class railway hazard model trained on RailSem19 and UAV-RSOD datasets, identifying sabotage iron rods, boulders, fallen limbs, barrels, and vehicles.
RailDrishti_Det_YOLO11m.pt
Step 04 REASON
Vector Spatial Engine
Converts track masks into continuous Shapely polygons, projects obstacle ground-contact points, computes lateral safety clearance, and triggers real-time ATP telemetry.
src/5_spatial_reasoning/geometry.py

04 — Physical Prototyping & Empirical Model Selection

Whitefield Station Miniature Testbed
& Architecture Selection

To empirically stress-test our v1.0 perception engine before scaling to full-track datasets, we engineered a high-fidelity physical miniature scale reproduction of Whitefield Railway Station. This controlled testbed enabled rigorous comparative evaluation across multiple candidate vision models under simulated lighting and physical obstruction scenarios to select the optimal architecture blend.

01 / Physical Testbed
Whitefield Miniature Scale Junction

Custom physical fabrication recreating multi-track junctions, sleeper geometry, platforms, and switch turnouts for continuous sensor prototyping, lens calibration, and camera mounting optimization.

02 / Model v1.0 Stress Testing
Controlled Optical Obstacle Evaluation

Live deployment of our v1.0 perception model under simulated physical foreign object debris, ballast displacement, and rapid rolling stock transit to test detection robustness.

03 / Architecture Selection & Dataset Scaling
Empirical Model Selection & Full-Scale Training

Systematic multi-model evaluation across candidate backbones to pick out and benchmark which architecture fits best (balancing latency, memory footprint, and edge compute efficiency) before committing to full-scale training on massive railway datasets.

South Western Railways (SWR) Field Visit

To validate our perception architecture against real-world operational environments, our team visited the South Western Railway (SWR) headquarters and divisional facilities. We formally demonstrated the Drishti-Kavach optical ATP engine to railway officials and submitted official proposals requesting permission to conduct live on-track sensor calibration, multi-spectral camera evaluation, and real-time obstacle detection tests across active railway stations, locomotive yards, and level crossings.
